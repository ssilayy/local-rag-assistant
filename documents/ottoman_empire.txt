The Ottoman Empire

Founding
The Ottoman state was founded in 1299 by Osman Bey as a small principality around Söğüt. Over time it expanded across Anatolia and the Balkans, growing into a vast empire.

Capitals
Throughout Ottoman history, three different cities served as the capital:
1. Bursa (1326-1365) — captured and made the first capital during the reign of Orhan Bey.
2. Edirne (1365-1453) — the capital moved here during the reign of Murad I.
3. Istanbul (1453-1922) — Mehmed II ("Mehmed the Conqueror") captured Istanbul in 1453 and moved the capital there. This date is widely considered the end of the Byzantine Empire and the end of the Middle Ages.

Greatest extent
The empire reached its greatest territorial extent in the 16th century, during the reign of Suleiman the Magnificent (1520-1566). During this period, Ottoman territory spanned three continents (Asia, Europe, and Africa), extending into Central Europe up to the siege of Vienna.

System of government
The Ottoman Empire was a centralized monarchy headed by the sultan. State affairs were discussed in a council called the Divan-ı Hümayun (Imperial Council). Provincial administration was organized through a system of eyalets (provinces) and sanjaks (districts). Under the devshirme system, young men were collected, educated, and placed into state and military service, including the Janissary corps.

Decline and fall
From the 17th century onward, the Ottoman Empire began losing territory and power relative to Europe. It entered World War I (1914-1918) on the side of Germany, and after its defeat its territory was occupied by Allied forces. On November 1, 1922, the Turkish Grand National Assembly (TBMM) formally abolished the sultanate, ending the Ottoman state — a 623-year-old empire. A year later, on October 29, 1923, the Republic of Turkey was founded under the leadership of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk.
